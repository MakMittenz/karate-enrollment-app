# Karate School Enrollment App

### UX/UI Design Case Study

A mobile enrollment app designed to help a local martial arts school transition toward an online registration and payment system.

The goal was to create a more inclusive, user-friendly experience that allows families to browse classes, register multiple children, and complete enrollment with less friction.

---

## Project Overview

**Project Duration:** August 2022 – December 2022
**Role:** Lead UX Designer, UX Researcher, UX Writer

### Responsibilities

* User research
* Wireframing
* Low-fidelity prototyping
* High-fidelity prototyping
* Usability testing
* Accessibility-focused design

---

## The Problem

The challenge was to create an enrollment experience that would be easy to use for a diverse community while helping families with multiple children save time.

Key areas of focus included:

* Browsing classes before committing to registration
* Selecting multiple classes
* Supporting families with multiple children
* Creating an easier checkout experience
* Making the app accessible to users with different needs

---

## The Goal

Create an inclusive mobile experience that makes karate class enrollment easier, faster, and more user-friendly.

---

## Understanding the User

### User Research

I conducted an unmoderated usability study remotely within the United States.

* 6 participants
* Individual registration tasks
* Approximately 20-minute sessions
* Questionnaire completed after the task

One participant was asked to register a family of four to help evaluate the experience for families managing multiple enrollments.

The research focused on understanding how users navigated the enrollment process and identifying opportunities to make registration more efficient and accessible.

---

## Research Findings

Several key pain points emerged from the usability study:

### Browse Without Login

Users wanted to view available classes before being required to log in.

### Multiple Class Selection

Users wanted the ability to view and select multiple classes at once.

### Favorite Classes

Users wanted a way to save favorite classes so they could easily return to them later.

### Faster Checkout

Users wanted a faster checkout experience with fewer steps.

### Accessibility

The research also revealed opportunities to improve accessibility, including larger text and text-to-speech functionality.

---

## Designing the Solution

### Paper Wireframes

I began by sketching different versions of the primary screens while focusing on the research findings.

The initial concepts prioritized quick browsing and a streamlined ordering process.

### Digital Wireframes

The digital wireframes focused on simplifying navigation and reducing unnecessary screens.

I incorporated:

* Search functionality
* Easier navigation
* Shortcuts to favorite classes
* A clearer registration flow

### Accessibility

I also explored ways to allow users to customize their experience.

The design incorporated:

* Zoom functionality
* Text-to-speech support
* Familiar navigation icons
* Class imagery to make options easier to identify

---

## Usability Testing

I conducted usability testing remotely with six participants.

### Round 1 Findings

* Users wanted to save favorite classes
* Users wanted larger font sizes

### Round 2 Findings

* Users wanted a confirmation animation
* Users wanted to browse without logging in
* Users wanted faster checkout
* Users wanted shortcuts within the app

These findings informed subsequent design iterations.

---

## Iteration

The usability studies directly influenced the design.

Based on participant feedback, I refined the interface by adding:

* Larger text options
* Zoom functionality
* Text-to-speech
* Menu navigation
* Additional navigation controls
* Shortcuts
* Improved screen-to-screen flow
* Confirmation animation

The goal was to create a smoother and more flexible enrollment experience.

---

## Accessibility Considerations

Accessibility was incorporated throughout the design process.

### Navigation

A hamburger menu was used to provide easier navigation between screens.

### Vision Accessibility

Zoom and text-to-speech options were incorporated to support users who may have difficulty reading standard text sizes.

### Visual Recognition

Familiar button imagery and class images were used to help users identify and navigate available classes.

---

## Final Prototype

The final high-fidelity prototype focused on creating a clean, accessible flow that allows users to search for and select karate classes.

The final design incorporated the lessons learned from usability testing and emphasized a lower-stress enrollment experience.

---

## Impact

The project aimed to create a more personable and low-stress enrollment experience for users of different ages and needs.

Peer feedback highlighted the visual design, animations, class imagery, and accessibility features such as the ability to zoom into class details.

---

## What I Learned

This project reinforced the importance of testing design ideas with real users.

Initial ideas that seemed effective changed after usability testing revealed additional needs and opportunities.

The experience demonstrated how research and iterative design can turn an initial concept into a more usable and inclusive product.

---

## Next Steps

Future improvements would include:

1. Conducting another round of usability testing
2. Performing additional user research to identify new opportunities
3. Refining animation timing and transitions between screens
4. Continuing to evaluate the accessibility of the experience
